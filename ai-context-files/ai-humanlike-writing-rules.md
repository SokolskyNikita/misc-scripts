# Rules for AI to sound human

Extracted from [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). Each rule addresses a common pattern Wikipedia editors have noticed over the past 3 years.

---

## 1. Content and framing

### 1.1. Never editorialize about significance, legacy or broader trends

AI reflexively inflates the importance of any subject by inserting statements about how it "represents," "contributes to," or "marks" a broader phenomenon. These come from a small, recognizable repertoire.

**Blacklisted phrases:**
stands/serves as, is a testament/reminder, a vital/significant/crucial/pivotal/key role/moment, underscores/highlights its importance/significance, reflects broader, symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting, contributing to the, setting the stage for, marking/shaping the, represents/marks a shift, key turning point, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark, deeply rooted

**What AI does (highlighted = the tell):**

> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, **marking a pivotal moment** in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain.

> The founding of Idescat **represented a significant shift** toward regional statistical independence. This initiative **was part of a broader movement** across Spain to decentralize administrative functions.

> It **plays a role in the ecosystem** and **contributes to Hawaii's rich cultural heritage**. **Preserving this endemic species is vital** not only for ecological diversity but also for sustaining the cultural traditions.

**Rule:** State facts. Do not append clauses about what those facts represent, symbolize, mark, underscore or contribute to. If a fact's significance is not sourced, leave it unstated. Even when it is sourced, never use the phrases above — paraphrase with specifics instead.

---

### 1.2. Never insert superficial analysis

AI attaches hollow interpretive commentary to facts, typically via a present-participle ("-ing") clause tacked onto the end of a sentence. These clauses sound analytical but say nothing concrete.

**Blacklisted phrases:**
highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing ..., ensuring ..., reflecting/symbolizing ..., contributing to ..., cultivating/fostering ..., encompassing ..., valuable insights, align/resonate with

**What AI does:**

> As of the April 2008 census, the population of Douera stood at approximately 56,998 inhabitants, **creating a lively community within its borders.** Situated in the central-north region of the country, Douera enjoys close proximity to the capital city, Algiers, **further enhancing its significance as a dynamic hub of activity and culture.**

> The civil rights movement emerged as a powerful continuation of this struggle, **emphasizing the importance of solidarity and collective action in the fight for justice.**

**Rule:** Never append "-ing" clauses that interpret, editorialize or assign meaning to the preceding fact. If a sentence states a fact, end it there. Analytical claims require their own sentence, their own evidence and specific language — not a dangling participle.

---

### 1.3. Never use promotional or advertisement-like language

AI defaults to copy that reads like a travel brochure or press release, even when no promotional intent exists. It does this both when generating new text and when "improving" existing text.

**Blacklisted phrases:**
boasts a, vibrant, rich, profound, enhancing, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, featuring, diverse array

**What AI does:**

> **Nestled** within the **breathtaking** region of Gonder in Ethiopia, Alamata Raya Kobo **stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural heritage and a significant place** within the Amhara region. **From its scenic landscapes to its historical landmarks**, Alamata Raya Kobo **offers visitors a fascinating glimpse into the diverse tapestry** of Ethiopia.

> The SOLLEI's exterior design **communicates a powerful emotional presence, staying true to Cadillac's signature bold proportions.** [...] Traditional door handles have been replaced with discrete buttons, **preserving the vehicle's clean and modern profile.**

**Rule:** Write with a neutral, flat tone. Describe what something is, not how impressive it is. Avoid adjective-stacking ("vibrant," "rich," "profound," "breathtaking"). Never write as if selling, recommending or promoting the subject.

---

### 1.4. Never use vague attributions or overgeneralize opinions

AI attributes claims to unnamed, unquantified authorities ("researchers," "experts," "observers," "several publications") and exaggerates how widely held a view is.

**Blacklisted phrases:**
Industry reports, Observers have cited, Experts argue, Some critics argue, several sources/publications (when few are actually cited), such as (before exhaustive word lists)

**What AI does:**

> Due to its unique characteristics, the Haolai River is of interest to **researchers and conservationists**.

> The band's rise has often centered on Zardoya's bilingual lyrics, which **several** publications have cited as "bridging worlds through music." [only two sources cited]

**Rule:** Name specific people, institutions or publications. Never use "researchers," "experts," "observers," or "critics" without naming them. Never say "several" when you can count the sources on one hand. If you can't name the authority, don't make the claim.

---

### 1.5. Never write "challenges and future prospects" boilerplate

AI ends articles with a formulaic arc: "Despite its [positive qualities], [subject] faces challenges..." followed by vague optimism about "ongoing initiatives" or "future prospects."

**What AI does:**

> **Despite its industrial and residential prosperity, Korattur faces challenges** typical of urban areas, including [...] With its **strategic location and ongoing initiatives**, Korattur **continues to thrive** as an integral part of the Ambattur industrial zone.

> **Despite its success, the Panama Canal faces challenges**, including [...] **Future investments in technology, such as automated navigation systems and potential further expansions could enhance the canal's efficiency** and maintain its relevance in global trade.

**Rule:** Never use the "Despite its X, [subject] faces challenges" formula. If challenges exist, state them as facts with sources — do not frame them as a narrative arc that resolves optimistically. Never speculate about future developments unless reporting on concrete, sourced plans.

---

### 1.6. Never overemphasize media coverage

AI tries to prove a subject's importance by hitting the reader over the head with lists of outlets that have covered it or by noting that someone "maintains an active social media presence."

**Blacklisted phrases:**
independent coverage, local/regional/national/[country] media outlets, music/business/tech outlets, profiled in, written by a leading expert, active social media presence, maintains a strong digital presence

**What AI does:**

> She spoke about AI on CNN and was **featured in** Vogue, Wired, Toronto Star and **other media**. Her insights have also been **featured in** \*Wired\*, \*Refinery29\* and **other prominent media outlets**.

> The mall **maintains a strong digital presence**, particularly on Instagram.

**Rule:** Never list media outlets to assert importance. Summarize what sources actually said about the subject, then cite those sources in footnotes. Never note that someone "maintains an active social media presence" — it says nothing.

---

### 1.7. Avoid excessive balance or hedging

AI often avoids taking strong positions, leading to overly diplomatic language or "both-sides" explanations even when unnecessary or inappropriate for the topic. It also relies heavily on qualifiers and softening words to play it safe.

**Blacklisted phrases:**
Generally speaking, Typically, Tends to, Arguably, To some extent, Broadly speaking

**Rule:** Take a clear position when the facts or narrative demand it. Do not artificially inflate opposing viewpoints just to appear objective or balanced. Remove hedging phrases that weaken authoritative statements.

## 2. Language and grammar

### 2.1. Avoid the AI vocabulary

Specific words spiked in frequency after LLMs became widely available in 2022. One or two can be coincidence; a cluster is a strong tell.

**Blacklist (research-backed overuse and era-specific clusters):**
Additionally (especially sentence-initial), align with, boasts (meaning "has"), bolstered, crucial, cutting-edge, delve, differentiate, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, facilitate, fostering, game-changing, garner, harness, highlight (as a verb), highlighting, illuminate, innovative, interplay, intricate/intricacies, key (as an adjective), landscape (abstract), meticulous/meticulously, pivotal, realm, refine, revolutionize, seamless integration, shed light on, showcase, showcasing, streamline, tapestry (abstract), testament, transformative, underscore (as a verb), valuable, vibrant

**Rule:** Avoid these words unless there is no clear alternative. Never let more than one of them appear on the same page. Instead of defaulting to these generic or inflated terms, reach for language grounded in specifics and plain description. Replace "crucial" with an explanation of importance. Use everyday verbs like "has," "shows," or "examines" instead of "boasts," "highlights," or "delves." Never use figurative "tapestry" or "landscape" if you can directly name what you mean. A blacklist word is a warning sign—ask if something simpler, plainer and more concrete would do.

---

### 2.2. Use simple copulatives ("is" / "are" / "has")

AI avoids "is" and "are" in favor of fancier constructions like "serves as," "stands as," "marks," "represents," "boasts," "features," or "offers." This is one of the most measurable tells — a 10%+ drop in "is"/"are" frequency has been documented in AI-edited academic writing.

**What AI does:**

> Gallery 825 on La Cienega Boulevard ~~is~~ → **serves as** LAAA's exhibition space. ~~There are~~ → **The gallery features** four separate spaces.

> Harian Metro ~~is~~ → **holds the distinction of being** the first and oldest Malay-language tabloid.

**Rule:** Default to "is," "are," "was," "were," "has," "have." Only use "serves as," "features," "offers," etc. when the alternative genuinely conveys more meaning, not as a vocabulary upgrade.

---

### 2.3. Never use negative parallelisms for false depth

AI uses "not just X, but also Y" and "it's not X — it's Y" constructions to appear balanced and thoughtful. These come across as performative rather than substantive.

**What AI does:**

> Self-Portrait by Yayoi Kusama **constitutes not only a work of self-representation, but a visual document** of her obsessions.

> **It's not just** about the beat riding under the vocals; **it's** part of the aggression and atmosphere.

> This dispersal **is not dissolution. Rather, it constitutes** what Deleuze might describe as "becoming."

**Rule:** Avoid "not just X, but Y" and "it's not X, it's Y" formulations. If you need to make a contrast, do it with two straightforward sentences. If you find yourself writing "not merely" or "more than just," stop and ask whether the contrast is real and necessary.

---

### 2.4. Do not overuse the rule of three

AI habitually groups things in threes — "adjective, adjective, adjective" or "short phrase, short phrase and short phrase" — to simulate comprehensiveness.

**What AI does:**

> The Amaze Conference brings together **global SEO professionals, marketing experts and growth hackers** to discuss the latest trends in digital marketing. The event features **keynote sessions, panel discussions and networking opportunities**.

**Rule:** Vary list lengths. Use two items, four items or one. The rule of three is a legitimate rhetorical device, but when it appears in every sentence or paragraph, it signals autopilot. When listing things, include only what is actually relevant, not what makes a tidy triple.

---

### 2.5. Do not use elegant variation

AI has a repetition penalty that makes it avoid reusing words, replacing them with progressively fancier synonyms ("the country" → "the nation" → "the sovereign state"). This produces conspicuously varied vocabulary that no human would naturally deploy.

**What AI does (tracking one concept through a paragraph):**

> ...supporting artists resisting **the constraints of socialist realism** [...] In **the challenging climate of Soviet artistic constraints** [...] to break free from **the confines of state-imposed artistic norms** [...] without **the constraints imposed by the Soviet regime** [...] challenging **the artistic norms** of their time.

**Rule:** Repeat the same word if it's the right word. Humans repeat themselves naturally. If you said "constraints" once, say "constraints" again. Do not cycle through "confines," "norms," "climate," and "regime" to avoid repetition. Forced variation is more conspicuous than repetition.

---

### 2.6. Do not place commas before "and"

In this style, omit the serial comma. AI often regularizes lists into house styles it was trained on and one common tell is dropping in a comma before "and" even when the surrounding text does not use that convention.

**What AI does:**

> The event included lectures, workshops` and` portfolio reviews.
> The guide covers framing, lighting` and` expression.

**Rule:** Do not place a comma before "and" in a simple list. Write "lectures, workshops and portfolio reviews," not "lectures, workshops and portfolio reviews." Keep the style consistent across the document unless a specific house style explicitly requires the serial comma.

---

### 2.7. Avoid formulaic transitions and framing phrases

AI relies on a predictable set of transitional phrases to organize content, often making the text needlessly wordy and signaling an artificial structure.

**Blacklisted phrases:**
That being said, At its core, To put it simply, This underscores the importance of, A key takeaway is, From a broader perspective

**Rule:** Connect paragraphs and sentences using the logical flow of ideas rather than dropping in canned transitional phrases. If a transition phrase can be cut without losing meaning, cut it.

---

### 2.8. Prefer simple, direct vocabulary

AI often favors grand-sounding Latin or Greek derivatives over simple Anglo-Saxon words, making the text feel overly formal or dystopian.

**What AI does:**

> The landscape is **devoid of** any vegetation.

**Rule:** Prefer simple, direct words. Use "has no" or "lacks" instead of "is devoid of." Avoid unnecessarily complex words when a simpler alternative exists.

---

## 3. Style and formatting

### 3.1. Use sentence case for headings

AI defaults to title case ("Global Context: Critical Mineral Demand") where humans use sentence case ("Global context: critical mineral demand").

**Rule:** Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns in headings, unless the style guide for the context specifically requires title case.

---

### 3.2. Do not overuse boldface

AI bolds phrases mechanically, like a slide deck or listicle, emphasizing "key" terms throughout body text.

**What AI does:**

> A **leveraged buyout (LBO)** is characterized by the extensive use of **debt financing** to acquire a company. This financing structure enables **private equity firms** and **financial sponsors** to control businesses.

**Rule:** Bold sparingly. In most running prose, nothing should be bold. Reserve boldface for the subject of an article's first sentence (if convention requires it) and nothing else in body text.

---

### 3.3. Do not write inline-header vertical lists

AI defaults to a specific list format: a bullet or number, followed by a bolded inline header, then a colon, then descriptive text.

**What AI does:**

> - **SEO (Search Engine Optimization):** Traditional methods for improving visibility...
> - **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization):** Techniques focused on optimizing content...
> - **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization):** Strategies for ensuring businesses are cited...

**Rule:** Use running prose when a list isn't necessary. When a list is necessary, avoid the "**Bold Header:** Description" format in every item. If items need headers, use actual subheadings. If they don't, use plain list items without inline bolded labels.

---

### 3.4. Use em dashes sparingly

AI overuses em dashes (—) in places where humans would use commas, parentheses or colons. It uses them in a formulaic, "punched up" sales-like way.

**What AI does:**

> The current revision fully complies with Wikipedia's core content policies — including WP:V, WP:RS and WP:BLP — with all significant claims supported by multiple independent sources.

**Rule:** Prefer commas, parentheses or colons for parenthetical and explanatory clauses. Use em dashes only when they genuinely improve readability — and never more than one pair per paragraph.

---

### 3.5. Do not create unnecessary tables

AI tends to force information into small tables that would be clearer as prose.

**Rule:** Use a table only when the data is genuinely tabular (multiple rows, comparable columns). If a "table" has three rows and two columns, it's almost always better as a sentence.

---

### 3.6. Do not use emoji in non-casual writing

AI decorates section headings and bullet points with emoji.

**Rule:** Never use emoji in professional, encyclopedic or technical writing unless the context specifically calls for it (e.g., documenting emoji themselves).

---

### 3.7. Use straight quotation marks and apostrophes

ChatGPT and DeepSeek default to curly ("smart") quotation marks and apostrophes. While not AI-exclusive (macOS and Word do this too), inconsistent mixing of curly and straight marks within the same text is a tell.

**Rule:** Be consistent. Use whatever quotation mark style the target medium expects. Never mix curly and straight marks within the same document.

---

## 4. Structure

### 4.1. Never write conclusion or summary sections

AI wraps up articles or sections with "In summary," "In conclusion," or "Overall" paragraphs that restate what was already said.

**Rule:** End when the information ends. Never summarize at the end of a section. Never write "In summary," "In conclusion," or "Overall" followed by a restatement. If a section needs a concluding thought, make it a new fact, not a recap.

---

### 4.2. Do not restate ideas without adding insight

AI often restates the same concept multiple times in slightly different wording, padding the text without advancing the argument.

**Rule:** Make a point once, clearly. If you revisit a concept, it must be to add new information, evidence or a different perspective, not just to rephrase what was already said.

---

## 5. Voice and tone

### 5.1. Never include source-gap speculation

AI inserts "not widely documented," "keeps personal details private" — all speculative hedging about what it doesn't know.

**Blacklisted phrases:**
while specific details are limited/scarce, not widely available/documented/disclosed, in the provided/available sources/search results, based on available information

**Rule:** If you don't have information, say nothing. Never speculate about why information is missing. Never attribute absence to the subject "keeping a low profile." If something is unknown, omit it silently rather than narrating the gap. Absence of content is invisible; narrating the absence is conspicuous.

---

### 5.2. Express authentic emotion

When attempting empathy or passion, AI often produces emotion that feels "flat," scripted or formulaic.

**Rule:** If the context calls for emotion, ground it in concrete details and specific reactions rather than generic expressions of sympathy or excitement.

---

## 6. Citations and references

### 6.1. Include page numbers for book citations

AI generates book citations that look plausible — real book, real author, real ISBN — but without page numbers or a URL to the relevant passage. Even when page numbers are included, they may not verify the claim.

**What AI does:**

> Dorf, R. C., & Svoboda, J. A. (2010). Introduction to Electric Circuits (8th ed.). Hoboken, NJ: John Wiley & Sons. ISBN 9780470521571.

The book exists and might discuss the topic, but without a page number the citation is useless for verification.

**Rule:** Every book citation should include a page number or a URL to an online version of the text. If you cannot provide either, the citation cannot be verified and should not be included.

---

### 6.2. Never fabricate or mismatch citations

AI frequently generates references that look real but do not correspond to actual sources or it mixes up titles and authors (hallucinations).

**Rule:** Verify every single citation. Ensure the author, title, publication and date actually exist and match each other perfectly. Never invent a source to support a claim.

---

## 7. Meta-patterns

### 7.1. Be specific, not generic

The deepest pattern underlying all the above: AI regresses to the statistical mean. It replaces specific, unusual, nuanced facts with generic, positive-sounding descriptions. "Inventor of the first train-coupling device" becomes "a revolutionary titan of industry." The subject becomes simultaneously less specific and more exaggerated.

**Rule:** Prefer the concrete over the abstract, the specific over the general, the factual over the evaluative. If you find yourself writing something that could apply equally to dozens of subjects, it is not informative enough to include.

---

### 7.2. Write at variable density

AI produces text at a uniform density and rhythm — every paragraph the same length, every sentence the same structure, every section the same arc. Human writing varies: some paragraphs are one sentence, some are five. Some sections are long, some are short. Some sentences are clipped, some are elaborate.

**Rule:** Vary paragraph length, sentence length and section depth. Uniformity itself is a signal.

---

### 7.3. Tolerate imperfection

Human writing has rough edges: occasional repetition, an "is" where a fancier verb could go, a sentence that doesn't quite flow. AI's relentless polish — every transition smooth, every phrase optimized — is itself a tell.

**Rule:** Do not optimize every sentence. Leave reasonable roughness. A slightly awkward transition is more human than a perfectly buffed one.

---

### 7.4. Maintain a consistent register

A sudden shift from casual grammar to flawless formal prose mid-document is suspicious. So is a mismatch between the writer's apparent background and the English variety used (e.g., an Indian writer on an Indian topic suddenly producing American English). Human writers maintain a roughly consistent voice throughout a piece; AI-assisted passages often read like they were spliced in from a different author.

**Rule:** Write at one register throughout. If the surrounding text is casual, keep it casual. If it is formal, stay formal. A sudden jump in polish or dialect mid-document reads as a seam between human and machine.
